\chapter{Production}
\section{Background}
The explosive growth of the Internet of Things (IoT) has led to the connection of billions of devices, continuously generating massive amounts of data. However, data processing at the edge (Edge Computing) faces significant challenges due to hardware limitations \cite{shi2016}. A specific characteristic of most IoT devices is their severe constraints in computational capabilities, storage space, and battery power \cite{imteaj2022}.
In this context, Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) play a pivotal role in monitoring traffic and protecting networks from cybersecurity risks, especially complex attacks. Recently, Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as an ideal decentralized solution. This approach allows multiple devices to collaboratively train a machine learning model without sharing raw data, thereby resolving data privacy issues and minimizing communication costs.

\section{Problem Statement}
Despite providing an effective distributed solution, deploying IDS in real-world IoT environments via Federated Learning still encounters core challenges. The first is the risk of privacy leakage: During the transmission of weights or gradients between devices, users' sensitive information remains at risk of leakage \cite{mothukuri2021}, enabling attackers to reconstruct the original data \cite{wei2021} and execute Model Inversion Attacks \cite{li2022}. The second is the Data Imbalance problem: In IoT network traffic, normal data accounts for an overwhelming proportion, while attack traffic samples are highly rare. Practical evidence from the UNSW-NB15 dataset shows that there are up to 1,064,987 normal samples but only 22,215 attack samples \cite{moustafa2015}. This severe imbalance degrades IDS accuracy \cite{aguiar2024} and directly hinders the feature extraction performance of deep learning models \cite{zhang2025}.

\section{Research Objectives}
To address the aforementioned limitations, this research proposes a comprehensive framework with the following specific objectives:
The FL model will act as a distributed model among Edge Devices, facilitating devices to transmit trained weights to the server to build a secure IDS for the IoT environment.
Integrate advanced secure computation methods, namely Secure Aggregation and Differential Privacy, into the weight transmission process to defend against data leakage attacks, ensuring the highest level of privacy.
The FL model will integrate the CVAE (Conditional Variational Autoencoder) model to generate synthetic attack data, helping to balance the dataset for the minority class, thereby making IDS training more efficient and accurate.

\section{Significance of the Study}
This research holds significant importance in simultaneously addressing two difficult problems in IoT network security: data quality and privacy. By integrating data generation to balance the minority class and applying a distributed training model accompanied by strict security mechanisms, this project provides a comprehensive IDS solution that completely protects sensitive information before data is aggregated.

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scope:} The project focuses on processing within an IDS in an IoT environment. The generation of balanced data is performed through the CVAE model combined with a distributed Federated Learning architecture at Edge Devices, along with weight protection mechanisms including Secure Aggregation and Differential Privacy.
\item \textbf{Limitations:} Secure computation and synthetic data generation mechanisms require a certain amount of resources; therefore, they may face challenges when deployed on resource-constrained IoT devices \cite{imteaj2022}. Furthermore, the evaluation is primarily based on standard offline datasets, which may not fully reflect the continuous evolution of real-world zero-day attacks.
\end{itemize}
